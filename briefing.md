# Acadia Retreat Briefing

This briefing covers the type 2 diabetes (T2D) clinical trial landscape and the logistics for our annual team retreat at Acadia National Park in Maine. All team members should read it before traveling. The source data below was pulled from ClinicalTrials.gov and the U.S. National Park Service (NPS) on October 20, 2026.

## Type 2 diabetes trial landscape

ClinicalTrials.gov currently lists **11,998 studies** matching "Type 2 Diabetes" as a condition. Because that exceeds the API's single-query analysis limit, the breakdowns below were computed across four disjoint start-date windows (studies with no start date recorded, 52 of the 11,998, are excluded from the tabulations, so the status table covers 11,946 studies).

Key takeaways: roughly two-thirds of the landscape is completed work (66%); 871 studies (7%) are actively recruiting right now; and 1,944 studies (16%) are in some state of active operation (recruiting, enrolling by invitation, not yet recruiting, or active-not-recruiting). About 14% terminated or were withdrawn.

Key takeaways: Phase 3 dominates interventional work (1,634), followed by Phase 4 post-marketing studies (1,400) — the field is heavily weighted toward late-stage evidence generation. Phase 1 (1,239) and Phase 2 (1,305) are roughly comparable, with only a small early-phase segment (86). Notably, the single largest category is "N/A" (4,481, roughly 38% of all studies), reflecting the large observational/registry literature that accompanies interventional T2D research.

## Acadia logistics

### Park overview

Acadia National Park protects the natural beauty of the highest rocky headlands along the Atlantic coastline of the United States, an abundance of habitats, and a rich cultural heritage. At 4 million visits a year, it's one of the top 10 most-visited national parks in the United States. Visitors enjoy 27 miles of historic motor roads, 158 miles of hiking trails, and 45 miles of carriage roads.

- Designation: National Park (park code: acad)
- Location: Mount Desert Island, Maine; park address 25 Visitor Center Road, Hulls Cove Visitor Center, Bar Harbor, ME 04609
- Phone: 207-288-3338 (TTY 207-288-8800); email: acadia_information@nps.gov
- Website: https://www.nps.gov/acad/index.htm
- Directions: From Boston take I-95 north to Augusta, then Route 3 east to Ellsworth and on to Mount Desert Island. Alternate: continue on I-95 to Bangor, then I-395 to US-1A east to Ellsworth, then Route 3.
- Weather: summer 45–90°F (7–30°C), fall 30–70°F (−1–21°C), first frost mid-October, first snowfall typically in November (average 73 in / 185 cm through April), winter 14–35°F (−10 to 2°C), spring 30–70°F.
- Operating hours: the park itself is open year-round, 24/7; check the website for facility hours (Hulls Cove Visitor Center etc.)
- Entrance fees: private vehicle $35 (7 days), motorcycle $30, per person $20, timed-entry vehicle reservation for certain locations $6, non-US residents $100, non-commercial group $20/person, Acadia Annual Pass $70.

### Events currently on the calendar

- **Drop-in Stewardship Volunteering with Friends of Acadia** (Regular Event) — Meets Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays from June through the end of October, 8:15 a.m.–12:15 p.m., weather permitting; no experience or reservation required. Work includes cutting back vegetation, clearing drainage, building bogwalk, tread work, and weeding carriage roads. Meet at the Hulls Cove Storage Facility near the Hulls Cove Visitor Center (Park HQ is under construction).
- **Wetland Revival – Walking the Path of Restoration** (Regular Event) — A mostly level 2.3-mile ranger-led walk through the Great Meadow on the restoration partnership between Acadia, the Schoodic Institute, and Friends of Acadia. Meet outside the Sieur de Monts Nature Center.
- **EeeK of Ecology Halloween Festival for Children** (Special Event) — October 24 at the Schoodic Institute campus in Winter Harbor; trick-or-treat through National Park learning stations, for children 12 and under with an adult.
- **Take Pride in Acadia Day** (Special Event) — November 7; volunteers rake leaves from carriage-road drainage ditches to protect them for winter, ending with chili, corn bread, cider, and cake.

### Campground options

Acadia has 4 campgrounds, all requiring advance reservations via Recreation.gov (the remaining walk-up/first-come sites are zero at each):

- **Blackwoods Campground** — East side of Mount Desert Island, ~5 miles south of Bar Harbor on Route 3; wooded sites within a 10-minute walk of the ocean. 281 sites (221 tent-only, 60 RV, 4 group); open seasonally, early May to mid-October. $30 tent/RV non-electric, $60 group tent area. Flush toilets, running water, dump station, camp store, laundry; no showers.
- **Seawall Campground** — West side of Mount Desert Island, ~4 miles south of Southwest Harbor (about 18 miles from Bar Harbor); all sites within a short walk of the coastline. 202 sites (43 tent-only, 59 RV, 98 walk-in, 5 group); open seasonally, Wednesday before Memorial Day to Columbus Day. $22 walk-in, $30 tent/RV/standard non-electric, $60 group tent area. Flush toilets, dump station, camp store; no showers.
- **Schoodic Woods Campground** — Newest campground, on the Schoodic Peninsula 1.5 miles southeast of Winter Harbor, ~60–70 minutes from Bar Harbor. 89 sites (13 tent-only, 41 RV-only, 9 walk-in, 2 group; 78 electrical hookups); open seasonally, Wednesday before Memorial Day to Columbus Day. $22 hike-in, $30 standard/tent electric, $36 RV electric no water, $40 RV electric and water, $60 group tent site. Flush/vault toilets, dump station, camp store, laundry; no showers.
- **Duck Harbor Campground** — Remote, on Isle au Haut (linked to the mainland by mailboat), 5 lean-to shelter sites; reservations open at 10 a.m. April 1 for the whole season, 7 months ahead. $20/night, 6 people per site, 3-night max, no pets, no generators, carry out all trash.

Reservation notes: 90% of Blackwoods/Seawall/Schoodic Woods sites are released six months in advance on the first of each month at 10 a.m. EST; the remaining 10% are released at 10 a.m. 14 days before the arrival date on a rolling basis. Reservations must be made through Recreation.gov, not at the campground office or in person.

## Recommendations

1. **Read this briefing before departure and comment on the pull request.** The briefing lives in this repository; the team review pull request is the place for questions, corrections, and additions.
2. **Weather and packing.** First frost is typically mid-October and the first snowfall begins in November, so pack layers, rain gear, and footwear for wet, cool conditions; fall temperatures run 30–70°F.
3. **Book camping or lodging immediately.** All four campgrounds require advance Recreation.gov reservations; 90% of sites are released six months ahead on the first of the month at 10 a.m. EST, with the remaining 10% released only 14 days before arrival. If the team prefers Schoodic Woods, remember it's 60–70 minutes from Bar Harbor. Non-campers should book Bar Harbor lodging early — this is a top-10 most-visited national park.
4. **Expect closures and plan around them.** Three areas are currently closed for repairs: the eastern section of the Beech Mountain Loop Trail (use the outer loop, Beech South Ridge, or Beech West Ridge to reach the summit), Schoodic Mountain Road (heavy equipment), and the southern section of Ocean Path from Otter Point to the southern terminus. Build hike plans accordingly.
5. **Build an off-site agenda around what's open.** Consider attending Drop-in Stewardship Volunteering (Tues/Thu/Sat mornings), the Wetland Revival ranger walk, or Take Pride in Acadia Day (Nov 7) for team bonding; the EeeK of Ecology festival is available for anyone bringing children.
6. **Use visitor centers strategically.** Hulls Cove Visitor Center is the main hub (daily 8:30–4:30 through October) — start there for maps and Island Explorer bus routing; note it involves 52 steps to the entrance with a separate accessible entrance. Sieur de Monts Nature Center is open through October 12 only.
7. **Budget for entrance fees.** Private vehicle $35 for seven days; the $6 timed-entry reservation applies to certain popular locations and doesn't permit re-entry; non-US team members should budget an additional $100 nonresident fee.
8. **Keep the trial landscape in view during planning.** With 871 studies currently recruiting in T2D and Phase 3 (1,634) and Phase 4 (1,400) dominating the interventional pipeline, our planning discussions should factor in both the active recruiting landscape and the heavily late-stage interventional portfolio, plus the large observational (N/A) literature that makes up ~38% of registered studies.
