## Type 2 diabetes trial landscape

Takeaways: about two-thirds of all T2D studies are already completed. The active pipeline is roughly 1,300 studies (871 recruiting plus 421 not yet recruiting). Attrition is real: 578 terminated and 242 withdrawn. The 1,536 "unknown" studies are largely older records whose status has not been verified recently.

Note: these phase categories account for 10,194 of the 11,998 studies; the remainder are records without an indexed phase value (mostly older entries). The largest single bucket is N/A, reflecting how much T2D research is observational/behavioral rather than drug development. Among interventional work, late phase dominates: Phase 3 (1,645) and Phase 4 (1,408) outweigh Phase 2 (1,313), Phase 1 (1,247), and Early Phase 1 (86) — a mature, treatment-heavy field with proportionally little early-phase exploration.

Study notes:

- **NCT07748208 (Hoffmann-La Roche):** open-label Phase 2 safety study of inavolisib plus fulvestrant, with or without palbociclib, in PIK3CA-mutated HR+/HER2-negative advanced breast cancer with type 2 diabetes. Target 40 participants.
- **NCT07878325 (Odense University Hospital):** Echo-CT-ProtecT-2-D — randomized comparison of an integrated Cardio-Metabolic Clinic versus usual care in T2D with established cardiovascular disease, including detection of unrecognized HFpEF. Target 375 participants.
- **NCT07409714 (University of Colorado, Denver):** SMART 2D — montelukast (a cysteinyl leukotriene inhibitor) versus placebo for renal and vascular function in adolescents and young adults with T2D. Target 50 participants.
- **NCT07868601 (German Institute of Human Nutrition):** LIME observational study of lifestyle, metabolic, and neurocognitive phenotyping to predict individual postprandial glucose responses across prediabetes, T2D, and healthy glycemic status. Target 300 participants.
- **NCT07868354 (Novo Nordisk A/S):** open-label tolerability study of switching from maintenance tirzepatide to zenagamtide in overweight/obesity with or without T2D. Target 60 participants.

## Acadia logistics

**The park.** Acadia National Park protects the natural beauty of the highest rocky headlands along the Atlantic coastline of the United States, an abundance of habitats, and a rich cultural heritage. At roughly 4 million visits a year it is one of the top-10 most-visited US national parks. Visitors enjoy 27 miles of historic motor roads, 158 miles of hiking trails, and 45 miles of carriage roads. The park sits on Mount Desert Island, Maine, is open year-round, and is headquartered at Hulls Cove Visitor Center, 25 Visitor Center Road, Bar Harbor, ME 04609 (phone 207-288-3338, acadia_information@nps.gov). From Boston: I-95 north to Augusta, ME, then Route 3 east to Ellsworth and on to Mount Desert Island (alternate: I-95 to Bangor, I-395 to US Route 1A east to Ellsworth, then Route 3).

**Fees and passes.** Private vehicle $35 (valid 7 days), motorcycle $30, per-person (foot/bike) $20, youth 15 and under free. Timed-entry vehicle reservations ($6, per vehicle) are required for Cadillac Mountain, and that road closes to vehicles at 9 pm. The Acadia Annual Pass is $70. Non-US residents 16 and older pay a $100 nonresident fee unless they hold an Annual or America the Beautiful pass. School/academic groups may qualify for an Educational Fee Waiver — worth pursuing if we register as an institutional group.

**Weather.** Fall temperatures run 30-70 F (-1-21 C); the first frost typically arrives in mid-October and snowfall begins in November, continuing through April (average 73 inches / 185 cm). Pack layers, rain gear, and warm sleeping gear.

**Events on the current calendar**

- **Drop-in Stewardship Volunteering with Friends of Acadia** (regular event): Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays through the end of October, 8:15 am-12:15 pm; meet at the Hulls Cove Storage Facility near the Hulls Cove Visitor Center. Trail and carriage-road maintenance led by volunteer crew leaders; no experience or reservation required.
- **Wetland Revival - Walking the Path of Restoration** (regular event): ranger-led, mostly level 2.3-mile walk through the Great Meadow to learn how the park, Schoodic Institute, and Friends of Acadia are restoring a damaged wetland; meets outside the Sieur de Monts Nature Center on 2026-10-21, 10-23, 10-28, and 10-30.
- **EeeK of Ecology Halloween Festival for Children** (special event): 2026-10-24 at the Schoodic Institute campus, Winter Harbor — trick-or-treat learning stations about national parks, creepy crafts; children 12 and under with an adult.
- **Take Pride in Acadia Day** (special event): 2026-11-07 — volunteers rake leaves from carriage-road drainage ditches to protect the roads for winter; ends with chili, corn bread, cider, and cake.

**Campgrounds (all reservation-only via Recreation.gov — no walk-up or in-person booking)**

- **Blackwoods Campground** — east side of Mount Desert Island, about 5 miles south of Bar Harbor on Route 3; wooded sites within a 10-minute walk of the ocean. 281 reservable sites (221 tent-only, 60 RV, 4 group). Season: early May to mid-October. Fees: $30/night tent or RV non-electric, $60/night group tent area. Amenities: flush toilets (seasonal), potable water, dump station, camp store, laundry, amphitheater, food storage lockers; no showers. Reservations: 90% of sites released 6 months in advance on the first of each month at 10 am EST; the remaining 10% released at 10 am 14 days before arrival, on a rolling basis.
- **Seawall Campground** — west side of Mount Desert Island, about 4 miles south of Southwest Harbor (roughly 18 miles from Bar Harbor); all sites within a short walk of the coastline. Season: the Wednesday before Memorial Day through Columbus Day. Fees: $30/night standard or RV non-electric, $60/night group tent area. Same 90/10 reservation release schedule; ranger station generally 9 am-8 pm in peak season.
- **Schoodic Woods Campground** — the park's newest campground, on the Schoodic Peninsula 1.5 miles southeast of Winter Harbor (60-70 minutes from Bar Harbor). 89 reservable sites with 78 electrical hookups (41 RV, 13 tent-only, 9 walk/boat-to, 2 group). Season: the Wednesday before Memorial Day through Columbus Day. Fees: $22 hike-in, $30 standard electric or tent-only electric, $36 RV electric without water, $40 RV electric and water, $60 group tent site. Amenities: flush and vault toilets, camp store, laundry, amphitheater; no showers.
- **Duck Harbor Campground** — remote, on Isle au Haut, reachable only by mailboat from Stonington; five lean-to shelter sites. $20/night, maximum six people per site and three nights, one stay per calendar year, all trash carried out, no generators, no pets. Reservations released 10 am on April 1 for the entire season.

## Recommendations

**Logistics**

1. Book campsites immediately. Every campground is reservation-only through Recreation.gov, and most close mid-October or at Columbus Day. Blackwoods is the practical pick for proximity to Bar Harbor; Schoodic Woods is the best base if anyone needs electrical hookups, but adds a 60-70 minute drive each way.
2. Cross-check retreat dates against the seasonal calendar. Going late October or later means limited campground and visitor-center availability, and winter information shifts to the Bar Harbor Chamber of Commerce.
3. Buy passes and timed-entry reservations in advance: $35/vehicle (7 days) plus $6 Cadillac Mountain vehicle reservation (road closes to vehicles at 9 pm). Non-US team members should budget the $100 nonresident fee or carry an America the Beautiful pass. Confirm whether our group qualifies for an educational fee waiver before paying per-person.
4. Route hikes around the three active closures (Beech Mountain Loop east section, Ocean Path southern section, Schoodic Mountain Road) and re-check NPS alerts the week we travel. Use the listed alternates such as Beech South Ridge Trail and Beech West Ridge Trail.
5. Fold team activities into the park calendar: the Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday stewardship volunteering (8:15 am-12:15 pm, no sign-up needed) and the ranger-led Great Meadow wetland walk are easy group options; Take Pride in Acadia Day (November 7) is a great full-team event if the dates align.
6. Pack for cold weather: fall highs of 30-70 F, first frost in mid-October, and snow possible from November onward.

**Science**

1. Cite the five newest recruiting studies above (NCT07748208, NCT07878325, NCT07409714, NCT07868601, NCT07868354) as the current-state snapshot of the recruiting pipeline.
2. Landscape framing: the field is heavily completed (7,883 of 11,998) with an active pipeline of roughly 1,300 studies (871 recruiting plus 421 not yet recruiting). Attrition (578 terminated, 242 withdrawn, 33 suspended) is a real planning consideration for any new protocol we propose.
3. Phase mix: N/A-designated observational/behavioral studies are the single largest bucket (4,495), and late-phase interventional work (Phase 3: 1,645; Phase 4: 1,408) outweighs early phase (Phase 1: 1,247; Early Phase 1: 86). The relatively thin early-phase space is a potential niche for novel-mechanism proposals.
4. Use these registry counts at the retreat to benchmark our own portfolio's status mix and phase mix against the field.
